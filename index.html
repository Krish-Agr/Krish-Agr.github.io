How I Work

My AI Product Framework

Developed through repeated delivery of production AI at Tier-1 institutions. Not theory — pattern recognition from real failure modes.

01
Ground truth before architecture
I spend disproportionate time in discovery — stakeholder interviews, failure analysis, data audits — before touching the technical design. Most AI products fail because the problem was never properly defined, not because the model was wrong.
02
Compliance as product skeleton
In regulated environments, EU AI Act and GDPR are not post-launch checklists. I architect compliance guardrails into the product lifecycle from sprint one — this is what allows high-risk AI systems to get risk committee approval rather than dying in legal review.
03
Measure what the business cares about
I define KPIs before building. Not accuracy benchmarks for their own sake, but metrics tied to business outcomes: escalation rate, onboarding time, processing cost, user satisfaction. A model that achieves 94% accuracy on a benchmark nobody uses is not a product.
04
Human-in-the-loop by design
Autonomous AI that can't escalate gracefully is a liability, not a product. I design HITL systems where the handoff is dignified — the AI knows its confidence limits and routes accordingly, creating a defensible audit trail and a better user experience.
05
Ship and observe, not ship and forget
Post-launch telemetry is part of the product scope, not an afterthought. LLMs drift. Retrieval accuracy degrades. User behaviour shifts. I design observability pipelines and continuous feedback loops into every deployment from the start.
06
Translate both ways
My engineering background means I can prototype fast, speak precisely with data scientists, and identify technically impossible requests early. My business experience means I can translate model capabilities into board-level business cases. This bilateral fluency is rare and valuable.
